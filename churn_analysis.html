1. Business Question

About a quarter of the customers in this telecom dataset (26.6%) left the company in the last month. This analysis asks three questions:

  1. Which customers are most likely to churn?
  2. What factors are associated with churn?
  3. How can the company target its retention budget?

2. Data

The data is the public IBM Telco Customer Churn sample: about 7,000 customers with their account details, services and whether they left in the last month.

3. Who Churns?

churn_rate_by <- function(data, var) {
  data |>
    group_by({{ var }}) |>
    summarise(
      customers  = n(),
      churn_rate = mean(Churn == "Yes"),
      .groups = "drop"
    )
}

7. Model Comparison

Recall is the share of real churners the model catches; precision is the share of flagged customers who really churn. For a retention campaign, missing a churner is usually more costly than contacting a loyal customer, so a lower threshold (0.3) is also tested.

evaluate <- function(prob, truth, threshold) {
  pred <- if_else(prob >= threshold, "Yes", "No")
  tibble(
    threshold = threshold,
    accuracy  = mean(pred == truth),
    recall    = sum(pred == "Yes" & truth == "Yes") / sum(truth == "Yes"),
    precision = sum(pred == "Yes" & truth == "Yes") / sum(pred == "Yes")
  )
}

p_logit <- predict(logit, newdata = test, type = "response")
p_rf    <- predict(rf, data = test)$predictions[, "Yes"]

bind_rows(
  evaluate(p_logit, test$Churn, 0.5) |> mutate(model = "Logistic regression"),
  evaluate(p_logit, test$Churn, 0.3) |> mutate(model = "Logistic regression"),
  evaluate(p_rf,    test$Churn, 0.5) |> mutate(model = "Random forest"),
  evaluate(p_rf,    test$Churn, 0.3) |> mutate(model = "Random forest")
) |>
  relocate(model) |>
  mutate(across(where(is.numeric), \(x) round(x, 3)))

8. Key Findings

  1. Contract type: Month-to-month customers churn at 42.7%, compared with 2.8% for two-year contracts.
  2. Tenure: Customers in their first year churn at 47.7%, the highest of any tenure group.
  3. Highest-risk segment (SQL): month-to-month customers who pay by electronic check and use fiber optic internet churn at 60.4% (1,307 customers), more than twice the overall rate.
  4. Charges: Churners pay higher monthly charges (median $79.6 vs $64.4), but the effect is not significant once contract type and other factors are controlled for (p = 0.30).
  5. Model: Lowering the threshold from 0.5 to 0.3 raises the random forest’s recall from 0.49 to 0.78, at the cost of precision falling from 0.64 to 0.52.

9. Recommendations

  1. Convert month-to-month customers: offer a discount for switching to an annual contract, targeted at customers in their first 12 months.
  2. Focus on early tenure: strengthen onboarding during the first year, when churn is highest.
  3. Target with the model: use the random forest with a 0.3 threshold to build a monthly retention list, accepting more false alarms in exchange for catching more churners.
  4. Review the electronic check experience: customers paying by electronic check churn at 45.3%, far above those on automatic payments (about 15–17%); encouraging a switch to automatic payment could reduce churn.

10. Limitations

  • The data is a public sample, and the results show associations, not causes.
  • The threshold of 0.3 is illustrative; in practice it should be set using the actual cost of a retention offer and the value of a retained customer.
